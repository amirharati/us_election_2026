# Wave and polling-error scenarios

Run `python run.py scenarios` or notebook
`05_wave_and_poll_error_scenarios.ipynb`. This uses the latest verified saved live
forecast; run notebook 04 / `python run.py live` first to acquire newer evidence.
No download, fitting, parameter selection or baseline promotion occurs here.

## Models and axes

Compare the main Gaussian Bayesian model (`repaired_both__selected`) with 20% and
50% corrected-polling mean blends. “Gaussian Bayesian” here means the packaged main, not a
new independent-state or reduced-complexity model. We retain its joint covariance.

The deterministic grid spans an additional Democratic wave of 0–10 **D-minus-R
margin percentage points**, and extra polling error of −5 to +5 points, in steps
of one. A two-point D vote-share gain at Republicans' expense would instead be a
four-point margin change. Scenarios are specified in margin units throughout.

- Wave `w`: an extra common final-margin shift **after** the existing forecast.
  It is not total change since the previous election, a wave already included in
  polls, or conditioning on a particular fitted national latent-factor value.
- Poll error `e`: additional **polls minus truth**, beyond the historical bias
  already corrected by each model. Positive error means polls overstate Democrats;
  subtract it from current polling evidence. Negative error favors Democrats.
- Historical priors, coefficients, bias estimates and evidence precision stay
  fixed. Poll changes can propagate to unpolled states through the fitted model;
  those states do not receive a fabricated direct poll observation.

This is a sensitivity analysis, not an estimate of the probability of each wave
or polling-error event. In reality the axes may be related; we do not estimate
that relationship or average the grid into a new forecast.

## Calculation

For prior covariance K, current observation selector H and observation covariance
R, retain the exact Gaussian gain

`G = K H' (H K H' + R)^(-1)`.

If current baseline posterior mean is m and covariance C, a specified error
vector e gives `m_new = m - G e + w*1`, with covariance C unchanged. Current poll
bias and noise are exactly the ones used in the saved live forecast.

For the corrected non-Bayesian helper, B maps poll shifts into its polling means.
Its current 30-day aggregate gives each observed state a direct multiplier
`firm_mass/(firm_mass + prior_strength)`; unpolled states have zero direct
response. The retained 2026 prior strength is zero. Historical error corrections
stay fixed. The script checks the selected half-life before using this response.

For helper weight a, define

`m_blend = (1-a)*m + a*m_helper`, `T = (1-a)*G + a*B`.

The scenario mean is `m_blend - T e + w*1`. It keeps the **whole Gaussian Bayesian margin
covariance C**, not only its marginal variances. A wave moves final margins
directly; a poll correction is attenuated differently by state and model, so the
two axes do not simply cancel point for point.

## Random stress

At SD 1, 2 and 3 pp, compare two extra zero-mean Gaussian errors:

- Shared: `e = sigma*z*1`, one draw for every observed state's polls. The added
  margin covariance is `sigma²*(T*1)*(T*1)'`.
- Independent: `e ~ N(0,sigma² I)` across observed states. The added margin
  covariance is `sigma²*T*T'`. Forecast responses can still be correlated because
  T propagates evidence across states.

These independent added shocks are **stress uncertainty beyond the baseline**.
The baseline already models election and polling errors. Adding these again is
not a recommended recalibration, nor a claim that baseline uncertainty omitted
all polling error. The deterministic scenarios and blend itself preserve C;
random stress deliberately increases covariance. Mean margins stay unchanged,
but state win probabilities, expected seats and control probability can change.

## Outputs and checks

- `seats.parquet` / `seats.csv`: all 471 model/scenario combinations at defaults.
- `states.parquet`: 35 state/contest rows per combination, margins, probabilities
  and 70% margin intervals.
- `poll_sensitivity.csv`: each state's mean response to one extra point of
  pro-D polling error, for every model.
- `scenario_heatmaps.png`: generated by the notebook; expected D seats and D
  control probability across the two axes.
- `run.json`: exact source forecast hash/cutoff/freshness, weights, grids and checks.

Marginal probabilities and expected seats use analytic Gaussian probabilities.
Chamber probabilities and central 70% ranges use 30,000 paired joint draws plus
continuing seats, with the retained D-control threshold of 51. Monte Carlo SE for
control probability is at most 0.29 percentage points. A displayed 100% in the
heatmap is rounded/simulated, not proof that an outcome is impossible.

Zero stress reproduces the stored baseline and blends exactly. The Gaussian
poll response is checked against a direct likelihood update and the saved
posterior covariance. Increasing a uniform D wave must not lower expected D
seats or simulated control probability. Random configurations retain the same
mean and use the explicitly augmented covariance above.

The first saved scenario analysis uses September 20 evidence, polls through
September 18, the flagged FRED cache fallback and September 17 political-control
carry-forward. It inherits all model/candidate/data limitations of that run.
